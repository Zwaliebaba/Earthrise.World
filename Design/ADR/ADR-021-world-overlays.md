# ADR-021 — The readability views are drawn as translucent voxel boxes through the voxel pass

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-11-01, by order M3-D2, which adds the overlay pipeline. Proposed the same day with the M3 plan ([MvpPlan.md](../MvpPlan.md), M3; [MvpPlan-M3.md](../MvpPlan-M3.md)). It records how the world overlays of [GameDescription.md](../GameDescription.md), *Readability*, reach the screen: the integrity view, the collapse preview and the passability view in M3, and the fog's last-seen land and the silhouettes in M4 if they fit the same path. [RendererLook.md](../RendererLook.md) §8 says only that they are drawn separately and never baked into albedo; [Hud.md](../Hud.md) §1 says they are drawn in the world, not in the HUD, and that the HUD toggles them. The owner gave it the go-ahead on 2026-11-01, as written (MvpPlan.md, M3, input 1; GameDesign.md, *M3's values*, item 1). Order M3-D2 implemented the engine's half and marked it Accepted; M3-F1 to M3-F3 draw the three views (AGENTS.md §6). The code forced one detail on decision 1: the overlay shares the voxel pass's vertex shader and its ray cast, but has a pixel shader of its own, `VoxelOverlayPS.hlsl`, which writes the palette colour unlit at the alpha a root constant gives, so that the opaque pass's shader, which the pass mark times, is untouched. A record's lightness offset still applies, so an overlay's records carry none.

Amended by order M4-G2 (2026-11-03), which adds the silhouettes, as decision 6 foresaw, and the engine's half of ADR-025, decision 8:
- **A silhouette is this pipeline with the depth test reversed** (decision 6): `VoxelPass::BeginSilhouette` draws a model's records flat in their palette colour at the caller's alpha, where the depth test fails, LESS under reversed Z, blended and writing no depth. The client draws it after the world and before the models: tested against the world's depth alone, a model shows its silhouette only where the world stands in front of it. Drawn after the models, a model's far voxels would fail against its near ones and tint it where it is seen. The models drawn after it cover it wherever they are seen. Input 23 sets the silhouettes' colours and alpha: the player's units and visible enemies, in their faction colours, at 0.5. *M4-H3* (2026-11-03) draws them: each unit's and each engine's model, its records flat in its owner's faction entry, from the update, which holds only the player's own and the enemies they see. Stones and open gates have none.
- **The fog's shade is not this pipeline:** it multiplies the world's own records where their column is not seen (ADR-025, decision 8, and `VoxelPass::SetColumnShade`).

Amended by order M4-I6 (2026-11-03), the owner's choice of the views across the Siege castle's thirty structures (MvpPlan.md, M4, input 23, as amended):
- **Each structure on screen has one line in the world**, through the interface pass, anchored a metre above its box's middle: its name, then FALLS while a collapse of it runs, OPEN yes once a route runs through it, or else its weak reading against its fall (input 22), and then `Locked · n s` while its repair is locked. Above 150 m of view height it is the name alone, but for the selected structure. Lines are laid nearest the screen's middle first, the selected one first of all and always whole. One that would cover a line already laid is laid as its name alone, and left out where that would too. All lie between the top bar and the deck. Input 23's eight full labels are not drawn: at 1280 × 720 they would bring back the crowding that M3-H1 removed.
- **The full reading stays in M3-H1's docked readout.** In layout A it shows the selected structure, else the one under the pointer, and nothing by default, so the selected one always reads in full. While the structure's repair is locked, the readout gains `REPAIR Locked · n s`, which counts down and does not flash. The breach lab keeps M3-H1's focus and its one readout, with no lines in the world.

Amended 2026-11-03, outside a work order, by the owner's choice of a fix after the breach lab showed the passability view over open ground far paler and flatter than its alpha:
- **Boxes of one draw that touch tint a ray once.** The passability view's boxes stand in the cell a figure stands in (decision 4), the one above the ground, so over open ground they make a layer a metre thick. The pipeline tests depth and writes none, so it blended every box a ray crossed. At the camera's 34 degrees and diagonal azimuth that is about 3.1 boxes, by arithmetic on the angles, so the view's 45% read as about 83% and hid the ground's light. The owner's screenshot agrees: its open ground, (149, 183, 98), is three blends of good over sunlit grass to within one level. Each overlay record now carries its joined faces, the faces that touch another box of the same draw, which the client works out for each layer (`Client::JoinedFacesOf`; `Engine::WithJoinedFaces`). `VoxelOverlayPS.hlsl` discards a hit that enters a box through one, since the ray crossed the other box first. A ray is therefore tinted once each time it enters a layer, in the colour of the nearest box, however many boxes it then crosses.
- **A stencil was set aside.** It would tint each pixel once, but the depth buffer is `D32_FLOAT`, with no stencil (ADR-011, decision 4). A stencil would have meant changing that format in every pass that uses it and in the render tests' readback, and the first box drawn, not the nearest, would have set a pixel's colour.
- **What is left:** a ray that leaves a layer and enters it again is tinted again, as one is over a step down in the ground, so such a step shows as a band at about twice the alpha. A box on a world voxel is unchanged: a face it shares with another box of its layer lies inside the world, and no ray reaches it. A silhouette's records join nothing, so a model's silhouette still blends once for each of its voxels that a ray crosses.

## Context

- **What the views show** (GameDescription.md, *Readability*; GameDesign.md, *Hypotheses*):
  - **The integrity view,** on by default: for each wall section, how close it is to opening, read from the collapse rule, as three readings: its widest hole against the width at which the stone above falls, its lowest opening against what foot soldiers can climb, and any gap under its foundation; the ground it stands on; its weak point; and the repair lock (M4).
  - **The collapse preview:** where a damaged piece will fall, before it falls, with an alert when it starts to go.
  - **The passability view:** where units can walk, climb and cross, and where a breach has opened a route. H2's "open" is this view's route for foot soldiers through the section.
- **Every reading comes from the simulation** ([ADR-004](ADR-004-rule-based-destruction.md), decision 5; [ADR-015](ADR-015-navigation.md), *Consequences*): the integrity readings and reach through queries, and a collapse's piece, strip and timing through the state update ([ADR-020](ADR-020-the-in-process-client.md)). The client computes none of it; it only draws what it was told.
- **The paper test fixed the integrity readings' form** ([Review/PaperTests/BreachPrediction.md](../Review/PaperTests/BreachPrediction.md), *The readouts*), with their keys and values in Hud.md §10's readout style, and found that the fall width must come from the resolver for each hole, not from a formula (its ambiguity 3). Hud.md §5's single OPEN percentage has no formula (its ambiguity 5).
- **The world is drawn by ray-cast voxel boxes** ([ADR-001](ADR-001-direct-voxel-ray-casting.md); [ADR-011](ADR-011-renderer-first-choices.md), decision 6): a record per visible voxel, bounded on screen by the vertex shader, hit exactly by the pixel shader, which writes depth. Models and debris go through the same pass.
- **What the camera hides** (RendererLook.md §7): at 34° the floors of ditches can be hidden, so a view painted onto the ground alone would vanish exactly where it matters. A view drawn as boxes in the world is seen wherever the world is.
- **The readings need text.** A section's three readings are words and numbers over the section; the interface pass ([ADR-019](ADR-019-text-and-the-interface-pass.md)) draws text on the screen.
- **Nothing may be baked into albedo** (RendererLook.md §8), and a chunk's records are rebuilt only on edits (ADR-011, decision 6), so a view that changed a record every frame would cost a rebuild a frame.

## Decision

1. **A view is a set of translucent axis-aligned boxes, drawn through the voxel pass's own pipeline with a blend state.** `Engine::VoxelPass` gains an overlay pipeline, `BeginOverlay`: ordinary `VoxelRecord` batches, each voxel drawn flat in its palette colour at an alpha the caller gives, blended over the scene, tested against the scene's depth with `GREATER_EQUAL` under reversed Z and writing none. A box placed on a world voxel therefore draws exactly over its visible faces, because the same shader computes the same hit and the same depth for the same box, and a box behind the world is hidden by it. No lighting, no corner shade, no shadow. The overlay pass runs after the opaque scene and the debris, at the scene's render scale, before the upscale.
2. **Overlay colours are palette entries of their own,** in the entries above the materials', beside the faction and ford entries the spike already uses there: Hud.md §2's tokens good, bad, accent, dim and text, converted to linear as every palette colour is. `Client/Look.h` names them. The palette's first 240 entries stay the materials' and are never used for a view.
3. **The client builds a view's records from what it was told, and rebuilds them only when that changes:** a state update that moves a collapse, an answer that changes a reading, a snapshot that changes the ground, a key that toggles the view, or the camera leaving a tile. A view's records are uploaded through the ring each frame, as the models' are, because they are few: a section's voxels, a strip's cells, the cells of the tiles on screen.
4. **What each view draws in M3:**
   - **The integrity view (F1, on by default):** for each structure the client knows, a label in the world through the interface pass, anchored above the section's middle and projected each frame: the section's name, then the paper test's three readings in Hud.md §10's `KEY value` form, HOLE, OPENING and GAP, each with its verdict, and GROUND. The weak point, the reading nearest its mark, has its voxels tinted accent. A section whose collapse is in warning or falling has its label marked FALLS. Hud.md §5's OPEN percentage is not drawn in M3 (MvpPlan.md, M3, input 5). *Amended 2026-11-02 by M3-H1, the owner's choice after the first lab run:* the labels in the world give way to **one readout, docked in the HUD** in the bottom-left corner, of the focused section: W2 by default, the section under the pointer while it is over one, and the section clicked last until a click elsewhere or Esc. Placed nearest the camera first, W1's label had taken the space above the enclosure and W2's was dropped, and the labels covered the enclosure the participant reads. The readout keeps the name and the paper test's words unchanged. HOLE and GAP each gain a bar of their width over the width at which the stone falls, in bad once it is reached, and none where nothing falls within the section. A row that changed flashes accent for two seconds. GROUND is dim, and bad only where the section can be undermined and a gap lies under it, since every section in the lab stands on earth and a red row on each read as danger everywhere. It is as wide as its longest row, at least Hud.md §3's 340, and lays out at a smaller UI scale where it would take more than 30 % of the screen's height. The weak point's tint stays in the world. Hud.md §3's readout place, right 416 and bottom 212, was not used: at the default framing it covers the trebuchet at 1280 × 720, and at a UI scale of 2 it starts left of the screen. OverlaysTests measures the readout clear of W2's box on screen at 1280 × 720 and 1920 × 1080 at each UI scale.
   - **The collapse preview (with F1):** a piece in warning has its voxels tinted bad, pulsing over the warning so that it reads as about to go, and its strip's cells, where units will be hit, tinted bad on the ground. The alerts W5 WILL FALL IN n S and W5 HAS FALLEN (Hud.md §10) are the HUD's, and they show whether or not the view is on; the debris of the fall is the v3 package's models, D1–D6, scattered over its landing (GameDesign.md, *M3's values*, item 16).
   - **The passability view (F2, off by default):** for the tiles on screen, every cell foot soldiers can reach from the player's squad tinted good, every cell they could stand in but cannot reach tinted dim, both as a box in the cell they stand in, so that a route shows as a path of boxes through the gap. The alert BREACH OPEN AT W5 (Hud.md §10) fires when reach first crosses into the courtyard. Other classes come with M4's selection.
   - **The attack cursor's strike mark (in FIRE mode):** the voxel the aim check says the shot would strike, tinted accent when IN RANGE and bad when BLOCKED, so that the player sees where a 45° shot lands before firing.
5. **The views are toggled by the HUD** (Hud.md §7): F1 and F2, each a key and nothing else in M3; the HUD model records which views are on, and the lab logs it with each answer.
6. **The silhouettes and the fog's last-seen land are M4's** and are not decided here. If a silhouette is a unit's model drawn where the depth test fails, it is this pipeline with another depth function; if the fog is a tint of the chunks' records, it is not, and gets its own amendment.

## Consequences for the code

- **`Engine::VoxelPass`** gains one pipeline state and `BeginOverlay`, with a render test on WARP ([ADR-012](ADR-012-render-tests-on-warp.md)): an overlay box on an opaque voxel blends to the expected colour at the pixels the voxel covers; a box behind an opaque voxel changes nothing; a box in the open blends over the clear colour.
- **`Client`** gains `Overlays`, which turns the HUD model's toggles, the state update, the answers and the snapshots into overlay batches and the labels' quads, and `Look.h`'s overlay entries.
- **The frame's cost grows by a few hundred boxes,** which the spike's measurements put far under a millisecond; `Earthrise` keeps the spike's GPU timer so that the owner can read the voxel pass's time in its title.

## What this forecloses

- Baking a view into a chunk's records or its albedo.
- A screen-space post-process for a view, in M3.
- A view computed on the client: every reading comes from the simulation through the boundary.
- Overlay colours taken from the materials' palette entries.
