# ADR-028 — The control: health-bar structures behind one seam, chosen by the scenario

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-11-02, by order M4-F4 ([MvpPlan-M4.md](../MvpPlan-M4.md)), which builds the seam, both rules and the control's tests; M4-F5 calibrates the hit points and M4-J3 draws the control's HUD. Proposed 2026-11-01 with the M4 plan ([MvpPlan.md](../MvpPlan.md), M4), its numbers the owner's input (MvpPlan.md, M4, input 19), and given the go-ahead on 2026-11-01, as written (MvpPlan.md, M4, input 1; GameDesign.md, *M4's values*, item 1). What the code fixed that the decisions left unsaid, as amendments (2026-11-02, M4-F4):
- **The seam has seven calls,** `StructureRule`'s: `ShotStrikes`, a stone's strike with its crush on units, its crater and its lock; `RamStrikes`; `TrenchDug`, a sapper's cubic metre; `Repair`, one tick of it on a structure a laborer tends at its stand once its lock has run out; `UpdateWorking`, step 10's shares and working; `CollapsesStructures`, whether step 5's support search may bring down a piece that holds a structure's voxels; and `MaxHitPoints`, the full pool its view gives. Both rules hold no state. `World::Rule` gives the one the trial's version names, which `SetTrial` fixes, so it is one for the world's life; a world with no trial runs the voxel version. `VoxelStructureRule` calls the world's M2 and M4 code, moved behind the seam unchanged, and the voxel version's 322 tests passed through it before the control's were added.
- **A shot strikes a structure** when the voxel it strikes is in a structure's box (`StructureHolding`), and its crush on units is the voxel version's. **A shot on terrain** craters as in the voxel version but leaves standing any structure voxel its crater reaches, and deals nothing: a pool takes only the shots that strike its structure.
- **A ram's blow is on stone** when the voxel it strikes is building stone or common rock; on the timber gate it takes the whole 100.
- **A trench's damage reaches** the sections within `repairLockReachVoxels`, 2 m, of the dug voxel across the ground, the reach that locks their repair (ADR-029, decision 2), which is input 19's "within 2 m of a section".
- **Every hit locks the structure's repair** for `repairLockTicks`, as a crater in its box does in the voxel version (ADR-024, decision 4), and has step 10 look at it as an edit in its box would, so its working and its events follow in the same tick.
- **At 0 a section falls straight down.** One collapse takes every voxel of its template that stands, through the resolver's `PlanCollapse` with its warning and its fall. Its cause is the piece itself, since a pool and not a crater brought it down, so no side lost it and the resolver's direction rule gives *down*: each voxel lands in its own column as rubble of its own material, and slump spreads the pile into the breach's ramp. M4-F5's trial in the control shows whether that ramp opens the section as the voxel version's does.
- **Repair** pays a cubic metre at each whole cubic metre restored, in the template's material: timber for a template more than half timber, stone otherwise, where the voxel version pays each voxel in its own. The storming clock resets when hit points restored bring the keep back above 0, as it resets when voxels put back bring it back past its threshold (ADR-026, decision 4, as the owner amended it on 2026-11-02); a hit point put back while it stays at or below 0 leaves the clock as it is.
- **A section at 0, which has fallen, and a gate structure at 0, which has lost its gate, are not repaired.** Their pools would come back with nothing standing: a section counted as standing with no voxel in it, or a gate that works with no gate to shut. A building at 0 stands and is repaired, the keep among them. The voxel version can put a broken gate's voxels back, so this is the one place where the control's repair does less; see *Left open*.
- **A structure's shares are its pool's:** its standing share, and its critical part's for one with a critical part, are its hit points over its full pool in percent, which the defender's commander repairs by (ADR-027), and a structure with a critical part works while it has hit points.
- **Decision 6's views:** `StructureView`'s `hitPoints` and `maxHitPoints` are 0 in the voxel version and the pool in the control. The HUD's half is M4-J3's.

Amended by the owner on 2026-11-02, after M4-F5's scripted attacker ran against the control (GameDesign.md, *2026-11-02: M4's phase F*, item 7):
- **Decision 3: a pool goes below 0.** A hit on a structure at or below 0 still takes its damage, down to minus its full pool, and a building works again only once repair brings it back above 0. Stones past a building's stop then cut it further from working, as the voxel version's cut its critical part further below its threshold. Before, the pool stopped at 0: M4-F5's attacker fired its six stones past the north flank tower's stop for nothing, a laborer had the tower working again 20 s after the last, and its archers killed the trebuchet on its way to its second stand. A section below 0 has fallen and a gate structure below 0 has lost its gate, so neither is repaired, as at 0. `StructureView::hitPoints` shows a pool below 0 as 0, so a health bar runs from 0 to the pool.

## Context

- **The control is the hypotheses' yardstick** (GameDesign.md, *Hypotheses*): the same stages with walls built of segments that have health bars, and resources from nodes that run out. It swaps the collapse resolver ([ADR-004](ADR-004-rule-based-destruction.md), decision 3) and the gathering rule and leaves the rest of the game unchanged. Both versions keep every cosmetic, and the integrity view is on by default, as the health bars are.
- **The HUD's differences** (Hud.md §1): health bars over wall sections, and HEALTH in place of PLATFORM on towers; everything else identical, and a test asserts it.
- **Siege has no gathering,** so the Siege control swaps the walls alone.
- **The balance targets bind both versions:** one trebuchet unopposed opens a standard section in about three minutes, nine shots at 20 s in the route table, and repair at most doubles it ([Review/PaperTests/SiegeRouteTable.md](../Review/PaperTests/SiegeRouteTable.md)).

## Decision

1. **Structures take hits through one seam,** `StructureRule`, with two implementations. `VoxelStructureRule` is M2's and M4's: craters, the support search, the resolver, and critical parts read from standing voxels ([ADR-024](ADR-024-structures-at-work.md)). `HealthStructureRule` is the control's. The scenario's trial names its version, and the world creates one rule for its life; nothing else in the simulation asks which version runs.
2. **In the control, a structure has hit points,** one pool a structure. `STRC` gives each template's (ADR-024, decision 7). An engine's shot that strikes a structure voxel deals the shot's structure damage and removes no voxel of it; a ram's blow deals the ram's, a fourth of it on stone; a sapper's trench deals its damage per cubic metre dug within 2 cells of a section's footprint, and the digging itself goes on, so the trench exists. Shots and blows on terrain crater as in the voxel version; the support search still runs, for terrain alone: structures stand while their hit points last.
3. **At 0 hit points:**
   - **a wall section falls whole,** through the resolver's own collapse, with its warning, its fall and its rubble, so the breach is the whole section's ramp, as a health-bar wall's breach is;
   - **a building stops working** and keeps standing, its garrison put out as in the voxel version;
   - **a gate's voxels are removed,** opening its passage: a gatehouse or palisade gate at 0 loses its gate and stops working, whichever of its voxels took the hits.
4. **Repair restores hit points** at the voxel version's rate per section times what a cubic metre is worth in hit points: the shot's structure damage over the shot's crater in the template's material, 50 a cubic metre for stone at first values (100 over 2 m³). A repair window therefore restores the same share of a shot in both versions, at the same lock and the same stone a cubic metre, so the economy of repair is the same.
5. **The structures' hit points are calibrated, not guessed.** Order M4-F5 measures on the voxel version, unrepaired, how many shots each template in the castle takes to open or to stop, firing from a stand at its weakest point by a fixed pattern for each template, and sets each template's hit points to that many times the shot's structure damage; the measurement is recorded in the commit and in `Structures.json`'s note. F4 starts from first values.
6. **The client draws the control from the same views.** `StructureView` carries hit points in the control and none in the voxel version; the HUD draws a health bar over each section with hit points, and HEALTH in place of the critical part's row on towers, the gatehouse and the keep (Hud.md §1 and §5, amended: Hud.md names the towers' row alone). A test in `ClientTests` builds the HUD model from the same state in both versions and asserts the two differ in those fields alone.

## Consequences for the code

- **`Simulation`** gains `StructureRule` and its two implementations; the shot's, the ram's and the trench's effects on structures call it. **`STRC`** gains hit points, and **`RULE`** each weapon's structure damage.
- **ADR-004's *Consequences*** say the resolver is "one interface with one implementation". The control is the second implementation the design asks for; ADR-004 is amended to say so when this is accepted. The resolver's collapse, which both rules use, stays one.
- **The trial's tests** run in both versions; the control's `SiegeAttacker` wins by storming as the voxel version's does.

## What this forecloses

- A control that differs in anything but how structures take damage and fall: units, AI, fog, the HUD and the cosmetics stay the same.
- A health-bar wall that falls in pieces: it falls whole, or stands.

## Left open

- **Rehanging a gate in the control.** At 0 a gate structure loses its gate's voxels and is not repaired (amendments above), where the voxel version's repair can put a broken gate back; the owner decides whether the control should rehang it when its pool is repaired.
- **Hold's control,** with its resource nodes and Hud.md's GATHER (M6; GameDesign.md, *Open questions*).
- **Damage drawn on a control wall.** Both versions keep every cosmetic, and a control wall shows no crater, since none is cut; the owner judges whether a health-bar wall needs a cosmetic of its own.
