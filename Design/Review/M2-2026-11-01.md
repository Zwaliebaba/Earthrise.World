**What was verified, and where.** Every suite was built and run in the Linux container with clang 18 and gcc, at -O1 and -O2, through a harness that stands in for MSVC and the native test framework, and clang-tidy 18 ran over every changed file with the repository's `.clang-tidy`. That is not the build AGENTS.md names. **CI's Windows job is the record:** MSVC Debug|x64, vstest, and clang-tidy 22 with the MSVC headers. Release and ARM64 were not built; nothing in M2 touches intrinsics, `/arch` or anything conditioned on the platform. Nothing in M2 draws, but B2, B4, B6 and D3 changed the renderer spike, which was not run (owner runs, below).

## What a breach looks like on the 1 m grid

The breach run stands on a dry, treeless stretch of the generated MVP map at seed `0x5eed2026`, whose ground lies between 8 m and 10 m, levelled to 9 m. An enclosure of ten T3 sections, 24 m by 20 m, stops the squad walking round; the target is the middle section of its south side. A trebuchet stands 40 m south, and six foot stand in a row 10 m south of the wall, ordered on tick 1 to a point 6 m inside.

What the rules did, tick by tick:
- **Tick 1.** The squad's search finds the goal out of reach and goes for the nearest reachable cell, (381, 179), against the wall straight below the goal. The squad walks there and waits, searching again after every rebuild.
- **Ticks 49 to 1649.** Five shots, 400 ticks apart. **Each is aimed at the bottom row of the south face and strikes the row above it:** descending at 45°, the shot meets the face a voxel higher than its aim point. The five stone craters open a hole 5 wide and 1 high through the wall's 2 m, a voxel above its base. The wall's bottom course stays, and it is masonry, which nobody stands on, so **the hole is not a route**.
- **Tick 1649.** The fifth crater leaves the slot above the hole's middle more than stone's 2 m span from the wall on either side, as E3's hand calculation says (ADR-004). It is 9 voxels: the column above the hole's middle, 5 m high through the wall's 2 m, less what the crenellation leaves out. It warns for 92 ticks, 1 s plus the time foot take to walk 5 m, with every voxel in place.
- **Tick 1741.** It falls, **east**: the fifth crater, at the hole's east end, is what took its support, and input 6 makes it fall towards that side. Its stone rubble lands over 21 ticks, and the strip under it crushes the wall's foot into rubble of its own material.
- **Tick 1762.** On the fall's last tick the rubble and the crushed strip make a ramp over the wall's foot at the section's east end. The squad, searching again after that tick's rebuild, finds its way through the next tick. The lead reaches the goal; the rear of the file stands on the ramp.

**What this says about the design's risks** (MvpPlan.md, M2's risks):
- **"Two shots one above the other open a route for foot long before anything falls" did not happen here.** It assumed the shots strike where they are aimed. At 45° a shot aimed at the foot of the face strikes a voxel up, so the hole's floor is the wall's own bottom course, which is not ground. A route opened only when the slot fell. A shot aimed at the ground just in front of the face might strike the bottom course; that was not tried.
- **The breach is a collapse, but a small one:** one slot of 9 voxels for five shots, and the way in is a rubble ramp at one end of the hole, not the hole itself. **Whether that reads as a breach is the owner's judgment, before M3 asks players to predict one** (MvpPlan.md, M2's risks).
- **It takes 1,762 ticks, 88 s, of which 1,600 are the trebuchet reloading.** With one engine at a 20 s reload, a breach is a minute and a half of fire.

## The budgets

Measured in the Linux container with clang 18 at -O2, not on the benchmark machine; the owner's run of `SimulationBench --breach-run` replaces these figures.
- **The breach run's 95th percentile is 0.001 ms.** It says little: 95% of the 2,401 ticks have nothing to do.
- **The costliest ticks of play are the fall's,** with its slump and its rebuild: up to 9.2 ms.
- **The slowest tick is tick 0,** which takes in the run's own levelling and enclosure as edits: 19 ms to 28 ms over three runs, over 25 ms in two of them. It is set-up rather than play, but it is a tick of the run.
- **A path search expands about 1,000 nodes a millisecond:** 33,077 nodes in 35 ms corner to corner across the MVP map. The budget of 4,096 nodes is therefore about 4.4 ms, and a cross-map search takes about 8 ticks.
- **Knowing reach** after an edit costs 0.46 ms with per-tile components, against 11.2 ms for region labels over the whole map (ADR-015, M2-F4).
- **The budgets stay as they started,** 256 voxels slumped and 4,096 path nodes a tick, Proposed in GameDesign.md, *M2's values*, item 13.

## Deviations, and what the owner is asked

- **One pull request for B to G,** by the owner's choice, in place of one a phase.
- **The C4 and D3 fixes** are commits of their own, as above, because a pushed commit is not rewritten.
- **The golden hashes** were computed in the container with clang and gcc and written into the test; CI confirmed MSVC agrees.
- **Reach is known from per-tile components,** the tile-level graph, kept for reach alone, which `SimulationBench` measured against region labels as order F4 asks (ADR-015). The search still runs over cells. This is the order as written, recorded here because it is the one choice F4 left open.
- **A search not done starts again after any rebuild** (ADR-015, M2-F4), which decision 5 did not say. Under rebuilds every tick a long search would never finish; the breach run's searches finish within a tick.
- **Cavalry and machines had no speed.** GameDesign.md gave foot's alone, so their figures searched but did not move. The owner has since given theirs, below.
- **Cavalry and machines could not cross the generated map:** the river's valley sides are steeper than their climb (ADR-015, M2-F3). The owner has since had the fords graded, below.
- **The decoration indices** 29, 30 and 31, an opening in timber, an opening in stone and iron in timber, are the implementer's choice of free palette slots (M2-B5).
- **`SimulationBench` reads the environment with `_dupenv_s`,** not `std::getenv`, which MSVC deprecates under /WX (M2-G2).
- **The work budgets** stay Proposed until the owner's bench run.
- **Whether the breach run's breach reads as a breach** was put to the owner, who answered below.

## The owner's answers (2026-11-01, after this report)

The owner answered the report's four questions the same day. Each answer is recorded where the design keeps it and was implemented in a commit of its own, after M2-G3:
- **The breach reads as a breach; the rules stay.** M3 asks players to predict the behaviour this report describes: craters that weaken the wall, and a collapse whose rubble makes the way in (MvpPlan.md, M2's risks).
- **Cavalry moves at 2.8 m/s and machines at 0.7 m/s,** twice and half foot's (GameDesign.md, *M2's values*, item 10). `RULE` gains both speeds, 9,175 and 2,293 raw a tick, so 14 m takes 101 and 401 ticks (`MovementTests`).
- **The fords' banks are graded 1 m in 3,** within machines' climb, under GENERATOR_VERSION 2, with new golden hashes (item 3). Every class now reaches the far bank along a ford (`GeneratorTests`). On the MVP map cavalry now crosses corner to corner, in 32,774 and 36,146 nodes. Machines are still short of the goal from both corners, hemmed in by the relief about their start rather than by the river (ADR-015).
- **The breach run is judged by its slowest tick of play,** every tick but tick 0, against 25 ms, and `SimulationBench --breach-run` reports tick 0 and the 95th percentile on their own (item 2; MvpPlan.md, M2's done-when; ADR-013). In the container that tick is a fall's, 9.9 ms.

**Owner runs added by them:** the renderer spike with `--generated-map`, to see the graded fords.
